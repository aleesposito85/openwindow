Why OpenWindow, and what v1 is

3 / 77

The problem

  • Basic single-glazed aluminium sliders lose heat fast: about 6 W/m²K through the glass.
  • Brush-pile seals leak air, so they're draughty in wind and let insects in.

What OpenWindow aims for

  • Double glazing: about 2.7–3.0 W/m²K at the glass.
  • Airtight when closed: EPDM compression seals, not brush pile.
  • An insect screen on the room side.
  • Open source and parametric: one small params file per opening; the generator does the rest.

Honest limits (2): not certified, not yet proven

6 / 77

Not certified

  • No AS 2047 window rating (wind, water, air): nothing has been tested by a lab.
  • Glass must follow AS 1288: toughened where it requires; buy it from a glazier.
  • Not designed for bushfire-prone (BAL-rated) sites.
  • Fit opening restrictors where the NCC requires them.
  • Check your local building rules before you start.

Not yet proven

  • Friction-stay gate: a 400 mm stay pair must fit a stack of ≤ 13 mm and a width of ≤ 14 mm, rated ≥ 15.7 kg. Many are 16–19 mm wide. If none fits, the CAD has to change.
  • Joint sealing at the 1 mm expansion gap relies on a ±50 % sealant.
  • Creep of printed parts in full sun, stays and corners under load, cam wear: all in the test protocol.
Build and test the bench window first (Testing section). Order glass only after measuring the real opening.

Structure: printed shells on continuous metal tubes

9 / 77
  • Every member has one continuous tube running its full length.
  • Printed segments, each ≤ 240 mm, slide over it. One stainless screw through the inside face fixes each segment to the tube at its middle.
  • Corner blocks take the tube ends in sockets; each tube stops 1.5 mm short of the socket floor.
  • The tube carries the load. The joint between segments only aligns and seals.

How it works

Segment joints are built to move

10 / 77
  • PETG and ASA expand by 65–95 µm/m·K. A 240 mm segment grows 0.8–1.1 mm over a 50 °C swing; one long printed part would bow instead.
  • Each joint is assembled at about 20 °C on two 1 mm shims. The generator shortens every segment by one gap per joint, so member lengths and tube cuts don't change.
  • One ring of 4 mm closed-cell foam cord sits in the 1.5 mm grooves of the two end faces.
  • MS polymer rated for ±50 % fills the band outside the cord and the end faces of the stops and fins: the gap cycles between about 0 and 1.3 mm.

Sheet smaller than the panes? Mullions and bays

21 / 77

Add "max_pane": [1200, 900] to the params file: the largest glass or acrylic sheet you can get, either way round.

  • The generator picks the fewest equal bays that make every pane fit, splits the window with mullions, and gives each bay its own fixed pane, awning sash, screen and two cams.
  • "bays": n asks for more bays than needed, never fewer.
  • A pane too tall for the sheet needs a second transom, which OpenWindow does not do: the generator stops and says so.
  • New parts: sill node SN, head node HN, transom cross-node TX, mullion shoes MSL, MST, MSH and segments ML, MU, each on a 40 × 20 steel tube.

Printer settings (Bambu P1S)

23 / 77
  • 0.4 mm nozzle (hardened steel for CF filaments), 0.2 mm layers.
  • 5 walls, 5 top and 5 bottom layers, 15 % gyroid infill. Most profile walls are 2.5 mm, so they print solid anyway.
  • ASA and PC-ABS: enclosure closed, door shut, no auxiliary fan, textured PEI plate with glue stick.
  • Exterior parts in your tier's material, interior parts in PETG (the parts list marks each).
Print the test coupon TC and one segment of each profile first, and check the fit before committing a full spool.

Heat-set inserts: 30 × M4 for this window

Pressed flush with a 230 °C iron, wherever a machine screw is re-tightened:

  • H: 2 each, on every head segment, so the hinges can move later;
  • TE: cam bases;
  • SBE: both boss pairs, 4 each (the keeper uses the outer pair);
  • CMB: the cam pivot boss;
  • TC: 2, for the heat and torque test.

58 / 77

Screen

  • The screen hangs on printed pin hinges (HG on the head, HS on the screen, an M4 × 50 bolt and nyloc as the pin): three on a wide window, two on a narrow one. Gravity and a matched pair of magnetic strips (A on the screen, B on the frame) close it.
  • Open it by the bottom rail only, and let it close gently. Don't hang anything on it or lean on it.
  • If light shows at the bottom corners, where the screen's magnet line leaves the transom, stick a thin foam pad there (test-log row 7).
  • If it won't close by itself, the hinge pins are too tight: back off the nyloc nuts until the knuckles turn freely.
  • To take the screen off, remove the three hinge bolts. To re-mesh it, pull the old 4 mm spline, roll in new mesh and spline, and trim.

Ventilation and weather

  • A top-hung awning sheds light rain when it is open a little: water runs down the outside of the glass and off the sash. Keep the opening small in rain.
  • Close and latch the sash in strong wind, storms or driving rain, and when nobody is home. The friction stays are sized for the sash's weight, not for gusts.
  • Water that gets past the outer weather seal drains out of the chamber between the two seals through the weep slots in the transom fin (TE segments). Cleaning

59 / 77
  • Glass and frame faces: warm water with a little dish detergent, a soft cloth or sponge, and a squeegee. No solvents (acetone, thinners, methylated spirits on painted parts), no abrasive pads or powders: they attack printed plastics and paint.
  • Acrylic glazing: use a plastic-safe cleaner and a microfibre cloth. Acrylic scratches easily, and ammonia or solvent cleaners craze it.
  • Replacement acrylic: cut new panels to the sizes in bom/<name>-glass.csv, in the same thickness as the window was built for (the glass list names it, e.g. "2 x 3 mm clear acrylic, 12 mm gap"). A different thickness needs re-printed beads and spacers.
  • Outside faces: clean the fixed pane and the outside of the sash from outside, from the ground or a safe ladder or platform. Don't lean out through the open sash.
  • Seal kerfs and seals: vacuum or brush out grit, and wipe the EPDM with water. Keep oils and petroleum products off EPDM.
  • Weeps: pull each weep cap (WC), clear the slot with a pipe cleaner, and push the cap back with its open end facing down. Brush out the transom fin slots with the sash open.
  • Screen mesh: take the screen off and wash it with soapy water and a soft brush, or vacuum it with a brush head.

Take off the sashes and screens on both sides of it, peel off magnet strip B (it covers the lock screw heads) and the EPDM bulb loops that run through its channel and kerfs, then unclip the beads and lift out both fixed panes beside it: a lower mullion's stop legs sit outside the panes. A frame part means taking the window out, so a small crack in a frame segment is better bonded and sealed in place.
